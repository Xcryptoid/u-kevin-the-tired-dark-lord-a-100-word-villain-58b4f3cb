A 100-word monologue, 542 characters: a fantasy villain greets the hero at the foot of his stairs and keeps putting the evil off until tomorrow.

> Come closer, hero. No, not that close. The stairs... I had them rebuilt steeper, so your knees would give out before your courage did. Clever, wasn't it? That was three centuries ago. The architect died. I forgot to replace him. My ledger of nine thousand souls sits open on the same page it sat on when your grandfather was a boy. I was going to collect them all tonight. Tonight, tomorrow, the ink never dries, the candles never quite go out. Sit. The throne is cold. I will monologue at you properly after I rest my eyes. Just... a moment.

The tiredness lives in the pauses and the deflections, never in a plain statement: the stairs he had rebuilt three centuries ago, the architect he forgot to replace, the ledger of nine thousand souls still open on the same page, the collection always due tonight and always slipped to tomorrow. He is still a villain with a scheme and a history, and the sting lands in the last line — the monologue itself is what he puts off.
